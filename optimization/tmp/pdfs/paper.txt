Bilateral Drive Gear—A Highly Backdrivable
Reduction Gearbox for Robotic Actuators
Hiroshi Matsuki , Kenta Nagano , Member, IEEE, and Y asutaka Fujimoto , Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—For safety purposes, cooperative robots are
installed with an actuator composed of a low-power servo
motor, a reduction gearbox, and a torque sensor. When
cooperative robots make contact with humans or the en-
vironment, they must detect the contact force with a force
sensor, a contact sensor, or a joint torque sensor. Equip-
ping these sensors increases the cost and size of the
application, but can be avoided under sufﬁcient backdriv-
ability of the actuator. To this end, we propose a method
that maximizes the power transmission efﬁciency of the
3K planetary reduction gearbox and develop a prototype
of the backdrivable reduction gearbox called the bilateral
drive gear. For this maximization, the proﬁle shift coefﬁ-
cients and the number of teeth are decided under some
conditions. The forward- and backward-driving efﬁciencies
of the prototype gearbox were 89.0% and 85.3%, respec-
tively, and the reverse-drive starting torque was 0.020 N ·m.
The drive efﬁciency of the same gearbox with uncorrected
teeth is 68.5%. The forward-driving efﬁciency was 20.5%
higher than the nonoptimized one. We conﬁrmed that pro-
totype gearboxes with different gear ratios are easily back-
drivable by hand.
Index Terms —Compound planetary reduction gearbox,
cooperative robot, robotic actuator.

A single motor in these robots cannot generate sufﬁcient
torque given the weight of the apparatus. Therefore, the motor
is combined with a reduction gearbox with a gear ratio between
1/10 and 1/300 [13], [14]. As the reduction ratio increases, the
reverse drivability (backdrivability) of the actuator decreases
because these actuation systems have more high-impedance
transmissions. Therefore, when deployed in contact situations
with humans or the environment, the robot must detect the
contact using a force sensor, a contact sensor, or a joint torque
sensor.
To improve the reverse driving performance of an actuator
with speed reduction, researchers have developed a series elastic
actuator (SEA) [15]–[18] and actuators with a built-in torque
sensor [19]–[21]. The SEA includes a low-rigidity elastic el-
ement between the output of the speed reducer and the load,
which measures the displacement under the load. While this
conﬁguration realizes accurate torque control (i.e., good force
control), it provides poor positioning accuracy. In addition, the
SEA cannot be passively backdriven when unpowered. In an
actuator with a built-in torque sensor, the performance of the
torque control depends on the sensor band, and the control band
is limited; moreover, the safety at the time of sensor failure is not
guaranteed. Both the SEA and built-in torque-sensing methods
require additional elastic elements and sensors, which increase
their cost. Another type of actuator employing a low-ratio gear-
box has been developed to reduce the rotor apparent inertia
and to improve the actuator bandwidth by increasing the torque
density of the actuator and decreasing the transmission ratio [22],
[23]. Reduction of the rotor apparent inertia is important for
human-safe robots in order to minimize the impact force.

by increasing the reverse drivability of the reducer itself, negat-
ing the need for additional elastic elements and sensors [24],
[25]. Table I summarizes the characteristics of various speed

reducer cannot have a large reduction ratio, it is often connected
through a plurality of stages. In this article, we investigate the
3K planetary gearbox, in other words, Wolfrom gearbox, which
is one of the compound planetary gearboxes. In general, this
gearbox has advantages of the compact structure and a large
reduction ratio [26], [27]. However, this efﬁciency decreases

other hand, harmonic gear reducers are lightweight, compact,
and have a large reduction ratio with high precision but low

power transmission efﬁciency, reduction ratio, allowable torque,
size, and backlash of the speed reducer [30], [31]. More specif-

decreases the power transmission efﬁciency. In fact, reverse
driving by a small speed reducer with a reduction ratio of∼1/100
is difﬁcult to achieve in conventional robot applications.
There are some papers that refer to the gearbox. Wang and
Kim explain the backdrivability of an actuator with a gearbox
by investigating the directionality of the efﬁciency of the spur
gears [32]. This article shows that the driving efﬁciency in 6:1
gearbox is 2% higher than the backdriving efﬁciency. Deb and
Jain optimized the design method of the multispeed gearbox us-
ing a multiobjective evolutionary algorithm [33]. By comparison
with these papers, we achieved the high backdrivability and the
high driving efﬁciency in the 3K planetary gearbox with a high
gear ratio by optimizing the total efﬁciency using the proﬁle shift
and the number of teeth.
Herein, we propose a method that maximizes the power
transmission efﬁciency of the 3K planetary gearbox. Prototype
speed reducers with reduction ratios of 1/50–1/380 are easily
operated in reverse drive, and the starting torque at the time of
reverse driving is also extremely small. The power transmission
efﬁciency of the 1/100 prototype speed reducer is approximately
89.0%.

III. T YPE-3K COMPOUND PLANET ARYGEARBOXES
This section describes the 3K compound planetary gearbox
shown in Fig. 3 . In our design method, we formulated the
forward-driving efﬁciency of this reduction gearbox. The com-
bination of teeth numbers and the proﬁle shift coefﬁcients of
the teeth that maximize the forward-driving efﬁciency were
determined by an optimization algorithm.
In this article, the gearbox shown in Fig. 4 is deﬁned as 3K.
The 3K planetary gear is one of the planetary gearboxes. This
gearbox is called Wolfrom gearbox too. It has a drive body, a
driven body, two axial adjacent ring gears, and the planetary
rods. The main rods are composed of three rods of the spur
gear (K means this rod). Fig. 4 shows a stick diagram of the 3K
compound planetary gearbox shown in Fig. 3 . The ﬁrst planet
gear P1 is mechanically connected to the second planet gear P2.
There is no sun gear in the second layer. The ﬁrst layer consisting
of S, P1, and R1 needs sun gear because S connects to the input

When we decide the speciﬁcations of the gearbox and conduct
the optimization process, the following steps have been taken.
1) Set the lower and upper bounds of the reduction ratio of
the target gear.
2) Set the minimum number of teeth for S and the maximum
number of teeth for R1 and R2.
3) Apply exhaustive search for the numbers of teeth, z
s,zp1,
zr1,zp2, and zr2.
a) Check the feasibility of the temporary solution for the
conditions for the gear ratio and the center distance
in the exhaustive search.
b) If it is feasible, optimize the efﬁciency w.r.t. continu-
ous variables using the quasi-Newton method in every
feasible solution in the exhaustive search. In practice,
xs,xp1, and xp2 are explicitly described by xr1,xr2,
andrc according to (81), (88), and (92).
c) Check if all the contact ratios are between 1.0 and
2.0.
d) Calculate the allowable torque of the gearbox based
on the tooth strength deﬁned by the standard of Japan
Gear Manufacturers Association and check if it sat-
isﬁes the requirement.
4) Find the best solution among the feasible solutions.
The maximization of the efﬁciency is equivalent to the min-
imization of the frictional power loss due to the meshing of
the gearing components. When we limit the upper bound of
the proﬁle shift coefﬁcient up to 0.9, the tooth shape becomes
reasonable, and the optimized efﬁciency degrades around 1%
compared to the case of the upper bound of 2.0.

V. S
UMMARY
We maximized the forward- and backward-driving efﬁcien-
cies of a type-3K compound planetary gearbox and tested the
performances of a prototype gearbox.
The prototype gearbox achieved 89.0% forward-driving ef-
ﬁciency, 85.3% backward-driving efﬁciency, and a 0.020-N ·m
reverse-drive starting torque. We also developed prototype gear-
boxes with gear ratios between 1/48.7 and 1/378.5. All of these
prototypes were manually backdrivable. Our bilateral drive gears
are expected to be employed in cooperative robots. However,
increasing gear ratio increases the apparent inertia [22]. As the
future work, we will investigate the effects of this apparent
inertia. In future, we plan to develop the actuator with this
bilateral drive gear for cooperative robots.
